No judges yet — that is the whole idea

A new panel starts by collecting. It accepts calls, stores every trace and judges nothing, so you can wire it into your agent today and decide what to judge from what actually happens.

Judges are written later, from an annotation pass over these traces. That order is not advice: it is what lets us say which traces and whose annotations produced each judge, which is the record alignment scores and the audit log both rest on.

What happens at 50

  1. Annotate. An expert reviews traces one at a time and writes what went wrong, in their own words.their work · M5
  2. Cluster. Those notes are grouped into a versioned taxonomy of failure categories, and each is marked as a deterministic check or an LLM judge.both of you, together · M6
  3. Author judges. Each category becomes one judge — a single yes/no question, with the model you pick for it. Each judge cites the category it came from.your work · M6
  4. Align. Each judge is measured against labels it has not seen, and either accepted or revised.both of you · M6

This is the part of the product that is a shared surface rather than a console: an expert supplies the judgement, you supply the wiring, and the versioned record of which traces produced which judge is what makes the result citable later.

Why this is here and not in the creation flow

Judges are authored from an eval pass (ADR-0061), so the picker lands with authoring at M6, in a panel’s Judges section. It is drawn here because its content is measured and perishable — cost, latency spread, effort consequences, what constraining precision costs in failover — and that work should not be lost between milestones.

Not part of what this mockup asks you to approve for M4. M6 decides how a judge is authored from a taxonomy; this is the model half of that screen.

Weight precision

Offered because glm-5.3-flash is open-weights and hosted by many providers at different precisions. Hosted proprietary models have no such variance, so this control does not appear for them.

anyDon’t constrain itMost failover, and consecutive calls may run at different precisions. fp8 onlyConstrain to fp8Consistent precision, far less failover.

What constraining costs. On 2026-08-30 this model had 20 endpoints, 13 of which could satisfy the rest of the pin; requiring fp8 left 6, because 7 of the 13 simply never declared a precision. Today it lists 27 endpoints — fp8 ×15, undeclared ×9, fp4 ×2, nvfp4 ×1 (pulled 2026-09-16T00:31Z). Constrain it when you have measured a swing, not by default.
